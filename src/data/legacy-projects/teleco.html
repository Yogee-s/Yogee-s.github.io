From camera pixels to eye contact

A camera only sees pixels. To make eye contact, Teleco needs two things: where you stand on its map, and how tall you are.

  1. 01Find people My YOLOv12 pose model marks where each visitor stands, between their ankles. → position in the image
  2. 02Place them on the map Kabsch alignment, calibrated by parking Teleco at a few spots, maps camera points onto its SLAM map. → x, y · 2–6 cm error
  3. 03Look them in the eye A curve fitted on Teleco's known height turns pixel height into real height. → height · <10 cm error

Why a camera in the room, not on the robot

A camera on Teleco only sees what is in front of it. One fixed camera overlooking the area sees everyone around the robot.

No blind spots

Visitors are tracked on either side of the robot, even when Teleco partly hides them from view.

Process once, share with every robot

Detection runs on one central computer, not on each robot. Results stream over MQTT (~5 Hz), so multiple robots can use the same detections.
